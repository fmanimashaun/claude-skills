The current §5 sentence ("Unhandled exceptions in requests and jobs are reported automatically") is wrong or misleading in four ways.
- Request exceptions are reported only when the exception has no `rescue_responses` entry and `show_detailed_exceptions` is false. A 404 or 422, and anything in dev or test, is not reported.
- The report is made after the error page has rendered, so the context and path describe the error page (A2).
- Retried job attempts are not reported (`report: false`), and a `retry_on` with a block swallows the final failure, so it is not reported. `discard_on` does not report either, unless `report: true`.
- The `context` hash carries the live controller or job object (A4), which is a data-leak risk if stored verbatim.

**B1 and B2: alert design**
- B1 is DESIGN-OPINION and defensible. Its factual parts check out in source: `deliver_later` only covers the enqueue, a failing mail job can be reported, `Rails.error.report(error, handled: true)` exists, and `handled: true` gives the default severity `:warning` (`error_reporter.rb:report`). The rest is design opinion:
  - a scan job with lookback longer than the interval;
  - a unique per-occurrence claim taken before notifying, which gives at-most-once delivery with the failure made visible;
  - an hour-keyed rate alert double-firing across a boundary (arithmetically true of any clock-bucket key).
- B2 is DESIGN-OPINION, a data-minimisation rule. It is consistent with A4 and A5: error classes, messages, arguments and ids can carry personal data. A spec that feeds forbidden strings and asserts none appears in any alert channel is a sound way to hold it.

**Skill may say**
- A1: "On Rails 8.1, `ActionDispatch::Executor` reports a request exception (`source: 'application.action_dispatch'`, `handled: false`) after `ShowExceptions` has rendered the error page, and only when the exception has no `rescue_responses` entry and `show_detailed_exceptions` is false. 404 and 422 style errors, and everything in dev or test, are not reported."
- A2: "The failing request's path and verb are in `request.env['action_dispatch.original_path']` and `action_dispatch.original_request_method`. If the exceptions app is a routed controller, `ExecutionContext[:controller]` is that controller and `request.path` is `/500`."
- A3: "`retry_on` reports each retried attempt only with `report: true` (default false). With no block, the final failure is re-raised and reported by the surrounding execution wrapper. With a block it is swallowed and only `retry_stopped.active_job` fires. `discard_on` reports only with `report: true`. The events `enqueue_retry`, `retry_stopped` and `discard` carry the job and `error:` in the payload."
- A4: "The `context` a subscriber receives includes `ExecutionContext.to_h`, which holds the live `:controller` and `:job` objects. `ParameterFilter` cannot see inside them, so extract only the keys you need rather than storing `context` whole."
