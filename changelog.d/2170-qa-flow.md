section: qa-flow
- **`check_ts_strict.py` reads a Next.js app, not only the e2e suite: `--scope app` — `plugins/qa-flow/scripts/check_ts_strict.py`** (#2170, slice 8 of #2162). The qa scope is unchanged and stays the default, finding for finding. The app scope reads the app's own TypeScript (everything outside `qa/`, `node_modules`, `.next` and the other build directories) and adds two rules to the three it already had: `ts-missing-option` (every tsconfig, merged through `extends`, must set `strict` plus `noUncheckedIndexedAccess`, `exactOptionalPropertyTypes`, `noImplicitOverride`, `noPropertyAccessFromIndexSignature`, `noImplicitReturns`, `noFallthroughCasesInSwitch`, `noUnusedLocals` and `noUnusedParameters` true, and `allowUnreachableCode` and `allowUnusedLabels` false, because `strict` includes none of them and an unset `allow*` is an editor hint, not an error) and `ts-directive` (`@ts-ignore`, `@ts-expect-error` and `@ts-nocheck` in a comment, with a description or not). The type-check step is `tsc` on the app (no `-p`, or a path outside `qa/` and `node_modules`) in CI and able to fail; a tsc on the suite does not satisfy the app and the reverse; `next build` is not accepted. The option set and the `allow*` semantics were checked by the doctrine-verifier on 2026-10-20 against typescriptlang.org/tsconfig (CONFIRMED); the `@ts-` ban is the maintainer's policy (#2162 decision 6), not a compiler fact. Not checked, and said in the docstring: a non-null assertion (`x!`), which needs a parser and belongs to the stack's ESLint rule. The selftest gains the app cases (every option in both polarities, `extends`, every CI shape, the exit codes of both scopes) and the mutation guard 17 more mutations (73 in all, all caught, on macOS and on Linux, `scripts/linux_check.sh`); wiring it as a gate is the `nextjs-stack` plugin's (#2165, #2166).
