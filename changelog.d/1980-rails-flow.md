section: rails-flow
- **The shared command normaliser is linear in the length of a line, guard-pr-ready.sh no longer takes quadratic time on a long command, and both it and guard-worktree.sh refuse an oversize one that mentions their word in time, with a reason — `plugins/rails-flow/hooks/scripts/lib/normalize_cmd.sh`, `plugins/rails-flow/hooks/scripts/guard-pr-ready.sh`, `plugins/rails-flow/hooks/scripts/guard-worktree.sh`, `plugins/rails-flow/scripts/check_hook_gates.py`, `scripts/mutations/hook_normalize_linear.py`, `scripts/mutations/hook_normalize_cmd.py`, `scripts/mutations/hook_guard_pr_ready_scope.py`, `scripts/mutations/hook_guard_worktree.py`** (#1980). Two causes, both measured on macOS (bash 3.2, BWK awk), and the second is the root. (1) `guard-pr-ready.sh` walked the command's lines with `${rest#*$'\n'}` slicing, quadratic on bash 3.2: 60 s at 158 KB and 83 s at 211 KB; it reads them with `read` now, the cure `guard-worktree.sh` already had from #1978. `guard-worktree.sh` was already linear on a plain-lines command, so the 16 s the issue measured did not reproduce there. (2) With python3 present, `normalize_segments` (shared by guard-bash, guard-pr-ready, guard-worktree and the qa-flow release gate) was quadratic in the length of ONE line: BWK awk makes every `substr(s, i, 1)` cost the length of `s` and copies the string on every `out = out c`, so a `substr`-only loop took 0.3 s, 1.2 s and 4.9 s at 100, 200 and 400 KB, and one 300 KB line took 13 s through the gates, past the 15 s hook timeout, which is a DENY since #1968. `_dequote` now splits the line into a character array once and builds its output in 2 KB pieces joined pairwise, the peel joins in pieces, and the ANSI-C decoder takes a run of ordinary characters at once: a 200 KB line costs 0.4 s in the normaliser (was 2.65 s), 300 KB through the gates 0.6 s (was 5.6 s), a 300 KB `$'...'` span 0.6 s (was 5.8 s). Its output is byte-identical to the old normaliser over about 30,000 fuzzed commands, in the plain and in the taint-marking mode (`NC_MARK_EXPANSION=1`). `guard-worktree.sh` dropped quotes with `${cmd//[...]/}`, worse than quadratic in the number of quotes (1,000 quoted words took 4 s, 2,000 took 35 s, 3,000 took 110 s: a 4 KB command stalled the gate); it uses `tr` now. Both gates refuse a command past 400,000 characters at once, with a reason, only when it mentions `worktree` or `ready` (quotes and backslashes dropped first); a large command that does not mention the word is let through unread, except one holding `$'`: a word built from an ANSI-C escape (`git $'\x77orktree' add`, `gh pr $'\162eady'`) is invisible to the dropped-quotes test, so past the cap a `$'` is refused too (fail closed). Still superlinear, below the timeouts: a line holding many `$(...)` takes about 6 s at 270 KB, through the three-level inner-string lexer. `check_hook_gates.py` gains a `normalizer_linear` group in part d (boundary cases for the 2 KB pieces and the 256-character slice, and a CPU-time ratio at 100 and 400 KB for four line shapes) and, per gate, a 160 KB plain-lines command, a 300 KB line, quoted words, a command just under the cap, and one over it with and without the word. The new guard `hook_normalize_linear` holds six mutants that put a quadratic habit back or break a seam; seven `hook_normalize_cmd` anchors moved to the new lexer. Maintainer decision: #1980 and 7d's review of #1992 (fix at the root, do not lower the cap); no framework claim.
