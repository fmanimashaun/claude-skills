section: qa-flow
- **A journey is measured as a flow: `journey-walker` emits `flow.json` and `flow_report.py` judges it against the budget the journey declares — `plugins/qa-flow/scripts/flow_report.py`, `plugins/qa-flow/agents/journey-walker.md`, `plugins/qa-flow/commands/walkthrough.md`** (#2250, slice 2 of #2221). `journey-walker` records one row per action (url, status, action kind, section, time to settled, spinner seen, forward actions, whether it is the declared end, whether it is a permission wall, and the form submitted with its field names and outcome), and `/qa-flow:walkthrough` runs the new judge on it and puts its ranked markdown in the report as a **Flow bottlenecks** section. `flow_report.py` (collector JSON in, findings out, like `layout_fit.py`) makes seven measurements: steps to complete, context switches, waits, dead ends, back-tracks, forms re-entered, and permission walls hit late. It refuses a journey whose own section of its journey document carries no `Budget: steps=N wait=Ns` line (optionally `switches=N`), exit 2: there is no default and no invented limit, and a project that wants NN/g's response-time limits cites them in its own budget after `doctrine-verifier` and `check_verdict_quotes.py` confirm them (not done here). A "late" wall is one at step 2 or later, with the steps spent named, because the plan's "when the first step already needed the permission" is not observable from a browser. Findings carry `rule: null`: none of the seven is the violation of any of the 60 framework rules (#2220), and the field is never filled by guesswork. 69 selftest checks (each measurement with its negative, the budget parser's twelve refusals, the schema's refusals, the exit codes) and a mutation guard of 28, all caught. `qa-reporter` files each finding with `Source` = `journey`. The version is assigned at promotion.
