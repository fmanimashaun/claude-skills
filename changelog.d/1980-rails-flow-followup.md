- **The #1980 gate fixtures no longer run every large row under a mutant, `--match` survives a busy machine, and a Linux-only survivor is dropped — `plugins/rails-flow/scripts/check_hook_gates.py`, `scripts/mutations/hook_guard_worktree.py`, `scripts/mutations/hook_guard_pr_ready_scope.py`** (#1980, from the Linux and macOS guard runs of #1992). Three findings. (1) The `guard_pr_ready` group keeps its subprocesses real under `--match` and counts only a release-gate run as a hook, so every large row of #1992 ran in full while a mutant slowed the gate: the slicing mutant took 10 minutes for an unrelated check and the guard timed out at the harness's 300 s limit. Each row returns at once when the harness is skipping, and the plain-lines row is 160 KB, not 100 KB, so the slicing mutant (about 17 s) clears its 8 s bound; at 100 KB it took 7 s and survived. (2) The #1645 labels embed `(rc 0, 0.1s of at most 21s)`, whose numbers differ between the survey and run passes of `--match` when the machine is busy: a Linux run at load 40 to 67 refused 3 of `hook_normalize_cmd`'s 40 mutants as "cannot be narrowed". `_stable` masks the timing text, with a meta check. (3) The mutant that puts `${cmd//[...]/}` back in `guard-worktree.sh` survived on Linux: that substitution is worse than quadratic only on bash 3.2 (macOS), and bash 5 does it in no time. It is dropped with a note in the guard file; the fixture row (1,500 quoted words) still catches the regression on macOS. Measured: `hook_guard_pr_ready_scope` 12 of 12 caught on macOS (623 s) and on ubuntu:24.04, `hook_guard_worktree` 6 of 6 on Linux, `hook_normalize_linear` 6 of 6 on both. Maintainer decision: 7d's review of #1992; no framework claim.
