- **A worktree teardown that drops its databases and stops its own servers — `plugins/rails-flow/scripts/worktree_teardown.py`** (#2058). `git worktree remove` ends a checkout and nothing else, so every worktree left its development, test and `_p<N>` shard databases behind (113 on one machine). The script refuses a dirty tree, an unmerged head, a locked worktree and the main checkout before touching anything; stops only the PIDs in the worktree's own `tmp/pids/*.pid` whose working directory is inside it (never by pattern); drops exactly the databases its own `config/database.yml` resolves to and their `_p<digits>` shards with `dropdb --if-exists --force`, never one the main checkout shares; removes the worktree without `--force`; and checks that nothing is left, failing closed when PostgreSQL cannot be asked. It has a `--selftest`, a mutation guard (`plugins/rails-flow/scripts/mutations/worktree_teardown.py`, 15 mutations) and `--check-wording`, a doctor gate that fails if the session-start line (`plugins/rails-flow/hooks/scripts/lib/worktree_guard.py`) or the handoff round (`plugins/rails-flow/scripts/wrap_round.py`) stops naming the script. Verified for PostgreSQL 13 or later (`--force` first appeared in 13; the 18 docs list `--force`) and current git, recorded in `docs/evidence/audits/2026-10-14-worktree-teardown-2058.md`; the design is the owner rule recorded on #2058 (2026-10-14).
